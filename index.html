01 / 05

Animal signals convey meaning

What if we could understand them? The sounds of nature contain boundless untapped information — in forests, oceans, and our own homes.

02 / 05

Can we interpret these signals?

Diana monkeys emit distinct alarm calls for the crowned eagle and the leopard. Other species — and now humans — heed the warning. We're only beginning to listen.

03 / 05

Can these signals inform our actions?

Animals detected the 2004 Tsunami hours before our systems did. Many fled to higher ground — and people who followed survived. Early detection saves lives.

04 / 05

Can we help them by listening?

Distress and pain alter how animals communicate. If we understand and monitor these changes at scale, we can direct resources to help — across wildlife, livestock, and our own companions.

05 / 05

When we connect, we act

The discovery of humpback whale song helped bring an end to commercial whaling. Understanding animal voices drives compassionate action. There is a world of information in animal communication — it's time we listen.

Our Mission

We integrate field biology, global acoustic datasets, and advanced data science to understand animal communication — grounding our work in what matters to animals and applying our findings to innovate welfare and engage communities.

Our Vision

A world where understanding animal voices drives compassionate action that centers welfare, guides conservation, and deepens our connection to the natural world around us.

Our Approach

What We Do

We leverage advancing technology and our strengths in biology, data science, ecology, and creative outreach to transform how we listen to, use, and connect with animal signals.

Interpret

Logan James, Research Lead

Understand animal communication signals through targeted field research and cross-species global datasets. We decode meaning from sound across birds, frogs, whales, and beyond.

Innovate

Lee Wall, Research Lead

Develop evidence-based technologies for health and welfare, using animal signals as indicators for early warning systems of disaster, disease, and distress.

Engage

Erin Wall, Research Lead

Partner with the public through creative outreach, community science, and open-source tools — making animal communication science accessible and meaningful to everyone.

Research

Current & Upcoming Projects

Bioacoustics

Birdsong & Human Music

Logan James

Exploring shared acoustic structures between birdsong and human music. Comparing species' songs, discovering musical parallels, and tracing the evolutionary roots of melody across the animal kingdom.

Active research

Marine Biology

Humpback Whale Song Development

Erin Wall

Investigating the development of song on feeding grounds, in partnership with coastal First Nations communities and conservation partners. Exploring how whale song changes over seasons and across populations.

In development

Conservation Tech

Detecting Disease & Distress from Frog Calls

Wavelength Team · Smithsonian Tropical Research Institute

Studying 15 species of neotropical frogs in the field and lab. Acoustic monitoring is cost-effective, non-invasive, and can assess thousands of animals simultaneously — validating a new welfare indicator and building an open-source early warning tool.
