Our Mission

We integrate field biology, global acoustic datasets, and advanced data science to understand animal communication — grounding our work in what matters to animals and applying our findings to innovate welfare and engage communities.

Our Vision

A world where understanding animal voices drives compassionate action that centers welfare, guides conservation, and deepens our connection to the natural world around us.

Why It Matters

Can these signals change our world?

Early Warning

Many species can sense disaster hours, days, even weeks ahead of human systems. During the 2004 Tsunami, animals fled to higher ground while our systems failed. Understanding their signals could save lives.

Welfare Innovation

Distress and pain alter how animals communicate, but we're limited in what we can observe. Acoustic monitoring offers a non-invasive, scalable window into animal wellbeing — letting us help them by listening.

Conservation Action

The discovery of humpback whale song helped end commercial whaling. When we connect with animal voices, we act. Understanding communication deepens empathy and galvanizes protection of the species that share our world.

Disease Detection

Disease alters how animals sound. If we can detect these acoustic signatures, we can identify and mitigate spread — across wildlife populations, livestock, and companion animals — before it's too late.

Only a small fraction of the species that communicate have been studied.

Technology is advancing faster than the research needed to guide it. We're here to close that gap — connecting biology, data science, and creative outreach to transform how the world listens.

Featured Research

Detecting disease and distress from calls alone

Acoustic monitoring is cost-effective, non-invasive, and can track thousands of animals at once. Our foundational project tests whether we can detect disease and stress in animals by listening — creating a scalable window into animal welfare that does not require capture or laboratory analysis.

Focus Species 15 species of neotropical frogs
Setting Field & lab at the Smithsonian Tropical Research Institute
Validation Acoustic analysis paired with stress hormone & disease samples
Application Open-source app applied to cross-species global acoustic datasets
Early detection → early intervention → saves resources and lives
